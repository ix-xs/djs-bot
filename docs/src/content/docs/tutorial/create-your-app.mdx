Before writing code, your bot needs an **identity** on Discord. This is done once,
in the Developer Portal.

<Steps>

1. **Create the application**

   Go to the [Discord Developer Portal](https://discord.com/developers/applications)
   → **New Application**. Give it your bot's name (this is what users will see) and
   accept the terms.

2. **Copy the Application ID**

   On the **General Information** page, copy the **Application ID**. You'll need it
   to deploy commands. It's a long number like `1234567890123456789`.

   <Aside type="note">
   The Application ID is public and harmless. The **token** in the next step is not.
   </Aside>

3. **Create the bot and copy its token**

   Open the **Bot** tab → **Reset Token** → **Copy**.

   <Aside type="danger" title="Treat the token like a password">
   Anyone with your token can control your bot completely. **Never** commit it to
   git, paste it in Discord, or put it in a screenshot. If it leaks, come back here
   and hit **Reset Token** - the old one dies instantly.
   </Aside>

4. **Enable the privileged intents**

   Still on the **Bot** tab, scroll to **Privileged Gateway Intents** and turn on
   what your bot needs:

   | Intent | Turn it on if you want to… |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Server Members** | react to members joining/leaving (welcome messages, auto-roles) |
   | **Message Content** | read message text (auto-responders, prefix commands) |
   | **Presence** | see members' online status |

   <Aside type="tip">
   `djs-bot` works out which intents your code needs and tells you at startup. If
   you forget to flip one of these switches, `npx djs-bot doctor` will say so
   explicitly instead of your bot silently failing to log in.
   </Aside>

5. **Invite the bot to your server**

   Go to **OAuth2 → URL Generator** and tick:

   - **Scopes:** `bot` and `applications.commands`
   - **Bot permissions:** start with `Send Messages`, `Embed Links`, `Read Message History`
     (add more later - e.g. `Kick Members` only if your bot kicks people)

   Copy the generated URL at the bottom, open it in your browser, and pick your
   server.

   <Aside type="caution" title="Why applications.commands matters">
   Without the `applications.commands` scope, your slash commands will never appear,
   no matter how correct your code is. It's the single most common beginner
   blocker.
   </Aside>

6. **Turn on Developer Mode (so you can copy IDs)**

   In Discord: **User Settings → Advanced → Developer Mode**. Now you can
   right-click any server, channel, role or user and choose **Copy ID**. You'll need
   your **server ID** in the next step.

</Steps>

## What you should have now

- ✅ Your **bot token** (secret)
- ✅ Your **Application ID**
- ✅ Your **server ID**
- ✅ The bot visible (offline) in your server's member list

Keep the three values handy - the next step puts them in a `.env` file.
